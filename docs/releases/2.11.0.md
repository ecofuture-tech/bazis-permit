# bazis-permit 2.11.0

Requires bazis 2.16.

## Security

- **The selector and the status of an `item.add` permission were never checked (every
  release up to 2.10.0).** A role with any `item.add` permission on a model created
  objects whatever their selector (`item.add.author`, `item.add.participants`,
  `item.add.work_order__engineer`) and, on a model with statuses (bazis-statusy), in any
  status its create schema accepted (`item.add.all.draft` created in `open`), skipping the
  transits and their validators, actions and permissions. The created item (also an
  included one, by the permissions of its own model) is now checked against the `item.add`
  permissions of the user once it is saved, with its to-many relations and the values the
  server filled (the author, the triggers), before the after-create effects of the route
  (`PermitRouteBase.hook_after_create`; an override calls `super()` first): a create whose
  item matches none is 403 `ERR_FORBIDDEN` and rolled back. The status segment is literal:
  `item.add.all.draft` creates only in `draft`, `item.add.all.all` in any status. The
  presence of an `item.add` permission still gives the create schema and `for_create`; an
  update is not checked against `add`. Each created item is checked right after its own
  insert (the main item, then the included ones in the order of the document): a selector
  that only an item created later in the same request would satisfy refuses the create
  (too strict, never a bypass). Effects of a create outside the database that do not wait
  for `transaction.on_commit` (a task queued directly, an external call) are not undone by
  a refused create. Mitigation on 2.10 and older: exclude `status` from the create schema
  of the routes of the models with statuses (`CrudApiAction.CREATE:
  SchemaFields(exclude={'status': None})`) and restrict the selectors with an
  `item.check.<selector>[.<status>]` permission (checked on the created item; it restricts
  the result of every update of the role too, so grant it only where the updates must
  keep the item in that selector and status).
- **The selector arrays could be read and written through the API (bazis-permit 2.2.0 to
  2.10.0).** The arrays `autogen_<field>_selectors` of a model with
  `autogen_selectors_fields` (an empty list means every selector) were attributes of its
  routes and of `included`, readable and writable: a user allowed to add or change an object
  could write his own or another user's id into an array and so grant the rights of that
  selector (for example `author`: view, change, transit) — an escalation of privileges.
  2.11 hides them from every schema (with the hidden fields of bazis 2.16: a write with
  them is 422, a filter or a sort by them is 400); the permissions still select the objects
  by them. On 2.10 and older, exclude the `autogen_*_selectors` fields in every route of
  such a model, its default route included (it builds `included`). Upgrade with bazis 2.16:
  on an older core the fields are not hidden.
- **A field hidden from the user could be found by trial with a filter or a sorting
  (bazis-permit 2.7.0 to 2.10.0; before 2.7.0 every hidden field).** The filter, the
  sorting and the search of a request (also through a relation of another route,
  `filter=vacancies__salary_min__gte=...`) reached every field the field permissions showed
  the user in some object, and every field as soon as an object could match no field
  permission. A role that views only some objects and hides a field in them under the same
  condition (a role for anonymous users: `item.view.all.open` with
  `field.view.all.open.__all__.disable` and `enable` of the public fields) therefore
  filtered and sorted by the hidden field, as the objects of the other statuses, hidden
  from it, match no field permission: `filter=salary_min__gte=500` and a binary search
  told the exact value of every object without a login. `PermitRouteBase.query_fields`
  now gives only the fields shown in every object: those shown by the field permissions
  without a condition, alone and with each conditional field group (a restriction that
  shows a field wins over one that hides it, so this covers every combination). A field
  hidden in some objects is 400 `ERR_FILTER` (`/query/filter`, `/query/sort`) and left out
  of the search and of `route_filter_fields`. The restriction (this fix and the
  workaround below) applies only with `BAZIS_FILTERS_STRICT` of the core on (the
  default): with `BS_BAZIS_FILTERS_STRICT=false` the filter, the sorting and the search are
  not restricted at all, every hidden field among them. On 2.10 and older, hide such a
  field from the role with a field permission without a condition as well
  (`field.view.all.all.salary_min.disable`), or remove it from the route (`fields`).
- **A list showed an object with the fields of one of its field groups (bazis-permit 2.2.0
  to 2.10.0).** `restrict_queryset` gave each object of a list the schema of the first
  field group it matched, in the order of the ids of the permissions (random UUIDs), and
  that schema had the field permissions of the group alone, without those that apply to
  every object. The card of the object merges the permissions of every group it matches
  (`enable` wins over `disable`), the list did not: with `field.view.all.description.enable`
  and `field.view.participants.title.disable` a participant saw in the list, depending on
  the ids, the title the rule hides from him, and an object of two conditional groups
  showed the fields one of them hides. Now an object of a list has the fields of its card:
  `_schema_key` is the combination of the conditional field groups it matches (one bit per
  group, in a stable order) and the schema of each combination has the permissions without
  a condition and those of its groups. Up to `FIELD_GROUPS_COMBINED_MAX` (4) conditional
  groups of a role on a model (2 ** 4 schemas); with more, every object of the list has the
  fields shown in all of them (as the filter, `query_fields`), never a field its card
  hides. The first member of the schema of the list (`schema_list`) is the union of the
  fields of the others, the columns of a client (bazis-front reads `anyOf[0]`); no object
  has it. The schema has only the combinations an object the user views may match: when
  the condition of each view permission is that of a conditional field group (a role for
  anonymous users with `item.view.all.open` and `field.view.all.open.__all__.disable`),
  the combination without these groups (every field) is left out, so the union does not
  name the fields hidden in the objects the user views. The conditions are compared as
  written, not by implication. A role that hides every field of a model showed every field (an empty list of
  fields was the default schema): fixed with bazis 2.16. On 2.10 and older, put all the field
  permissions of a role on a model under one condition (or none): one field group.
- **`disable` on change did not protect a field required in the model (bazis-permit
  2.2.0 to 2.10.0, with bazis 2.2.0 to 2.15.1).** The core kept a field required in the
  model in the update schema whatever `field.change.<selector>.<field>.disable` (or
  `__all__.disable`) said, so a role the permission should stop changed the field by an
  update, by the relationships endpoints of a relation and in the `included` of an
  update. With bazis 2.16 the update schema hides a field its own `disable` (or
  `__all__.disable`) hides whatever its requiredness in the model, except a field whose
  own `required` keeps it against `__all__.disable` (the create schema still keeps a
  required field; the guide, Field permissions, has the rule): an update with it is 422,
  the relationships endpoints answer 403
  `ERR_RELATIONSHIP_READONLY`. Upgrade with bazis 2.16: on an older core the field stays
  writable. On older versions declare the field `readonly` on change
  (`field.change.<selector>.<field>.readonly`: an update ignores it).

- **A field permission of add with a condition did nothing (bazis-permit 2.2.0 to
  2.10.0).** `field.add.<selector>.<field>.<restriction>` with a selector other than `all`,
  and on a model with statuses (bazis-statusy) `field.add.<selector>.<status>.…` with a
  status other than `all` (the initial status, `draft`, too), was left out of the create
  schema without a word: the create schema is built before the object exists, so its
  condition cannot be evaluated (and the CREATE schema of a model with statuses accepts an
  initial `status`). A `disable` or a `readonly` declared so left the field writable on a
  create: a role that should not approve with `field.add.all.draft.is_approved.disable`
  created approved objects. 2.11 refuses such a declaration in `roles.py` (`permit.E006`,
  the hint names the slug to write; for `enable`, which leaves the create stricter than
  declared, the warning `permit.W011`) and `bazis_doctor` reports such a permission of a role of the
  database (`permit.W010`); a create itself is unchanged (the item permissions of add
  restrict which objects a role creates, see above). On 2.10
  and older, write the condition `all` (`field.add.all.all.<field>.disable`, without
  statuses `field.add.all.<field>.disable`): it applies to every create of the role.

## Breaking changes

- **A create is refused when its item matches no `item.add` permission** (see Security):
  a role whose `item.add` permissions on a model with statuses name a status other than
  the initial one refuses a create without `status` (bazis-statusy 2.12 reports it,
  `statusy.W005`); a selector the new item cannot match (a reverse relation nobody links
  yet) refuses every create of the role. Check the roles of a product against the objects
  it creates, and grant `item.add.all` (`item.add.all.all`) where the selector or the
  status must not restrict a create.
- **A field permission of add with a condition that restricts its field stops `migrate`**
  (`permit.E006`, see Security: `disable`, `readonly`, `filter:`): `apply_declarations`
  refuses the declarations, as for `permit.E005`. Replace the condition by `all` (the hint
  names the slug), or remove the permission if the restriction must not apply to every
  create of the role. One that only shows its field (`enable`) is the warning
  `permit.W011`.

- **Custom actions declare who may call them.** A custom action of a `PermitRouteBase` (a
  route of kind `other`: the `http_*` decorators without `kind=`, not the internal route) is
  checked in `route_check` of the core, before it runs:
  - `public=True`: anyone, limited by `BAZIS_THROTTLE_PUBLIC` of the core (per user or IP
    address);
  - `access_action=`: the roles decide, also those for anonymous users; an action of the
    collection needs the permission on the model (403), an action of an item on its item,
    which bazis-permit takes by `item_id` into `self.item` before the action runs (404 for
    an item the user cannot view, 403 without the permission), locked for POST, PUT, PATCH
    and DELETE so that the permission checked holds while the action runs;
  - neither: authenticated users only, an anonymous user gets 403 `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (as on a
    create or an update), whatever the roles for anonymous users.

  The item of such a write action is locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` for the whole action, so
  long actions on the same hot row wait for each other.

  The `/_id/` route of the core declares `access_action=view`: it is open as `GET /`, also
  to the roles for anonymous users.
- **The selector arrays are not in the schemas.** The generated fields
  `autogen_<field>_selectors` (`autogen_selectors_fields`) were attributes of every route
  of the model and of its objects in `included`: a client read the ids in them and a user
  who may change an object (or create one) could write the id of another user into an
  array, which gave that user the permissions of the selector (`author`) on the object.
  `PermitModelMixin.get_fields_hidden()` (bazis 2.16) leaves them out of the default
  schemas: a write with them is 422 `ERR_VALIDATE`, a `filter` or a `sort` by them is 400.
  They still select the objects for the permissions; the project fills them (or the
  admin). A route that shows one names it in `include`; the `exclude` of a route that
  removed them can go.
- **A field the field permissions hide in some objects is not filterable, sortable or
  searchable** (see Security), also when the user views only the objects that show it: a
  rule hiding it in a status he cannot view (`field.view.all.archived.salary.disable` with
  `item.view.all.open`) closes it. Limit such a rule to the roles that view that status.
- **`permit.W004` is removed.** The untranslated names of the declared roles and groups
  are listed by `manage.py bazis_messages status` of the core; the check printed them on
  every command, also into the JSON of `bazis_messages`.

## Changes

- **`permit.W007`**: a custom action, of the collection or of an item, that declares
  neither `access_action` nor `public` (open to every authenticated user); `public=False`
  declares that intent.
  The check needs the loaded application, so `manage.py bazis_doctor` reports it, not a
  plain `manage.py check`: run `bazis_doctor` after the upgrade.
- **`permit.W008`**: a custom action (not public) whose `access_action` a role for
  anonymous users has, declared in `roles.py` or, with the database checks, in the
  database: anonymous users call it. An aggregate or a report with
  `access_action=CrudAccessAction.VIEW` is open to everyone who views the model (a guest
  read the funnel of every open vacancy); declare `public=True` if anonymous users may
  call it, else an operation of its own (an `AccessAction` subclass) granted only to the
  roles that may see it. The ids of the list (`/_id/`) are not reported. An action of an
  item is reported for a permission an anonymous user may match: `all`, or a condition
  that is not only `__selector__` (one with an alternative or a negation,
  `author=__selector__|is_public=true`, is reported without being evaluated). The guide has
  the recipe: an operation of its own, and the objects counted by the `restrict_queryset`
  of their default route with the user of the request.
- **`permit.W009`**: a field permission of `roles.py` that disables a required field on
  `add` (`<app>.<model>.field.add.all.<field>.disable`, or `__all__` without an
  `enable` of the field) hides nothing: the core keeps a field required to create an item
  in the create schema, and a create without it is 422. Fill the field on the server
  (make it optional in the model and set it in `hook_before_create`); the guide has the
  rule, in its new section "Field permissions". A `disable` on `change` is not reported:
  with bazis 2.16 it hides any field, nor one on `add` with a condition (`permit.E006`).
- **`permit.W010`** (`bazis_doctor`, with the database): a field permission of add with a
  condition that a role of the database has (made in the admin or the API, not declared),
  with the roles and the slug; it never applies (see Security, `permit.E006`).
- **`permit.W011`**: a field permission of add with a condition that only shows its field
  (`enable`) in `roles.py`: it never applies, the create stays stricter than declared;
  remove it, or write the condition `all` only if every create of the role may write the
  field.
- The guide is read by sections: its introduction maps them (bazis-mcp serves it alone),
  and the field permissions have a section of their own; the manifest items are one line
  each, their details in the guide.
- **The meta field `custom_actions`** (`?meta=custom_actions`): the names of the custom
  actions (`action` of `x-bazis`) the user may call, by the rule of `route_check`
  (`PermitRouteBase.action_allowed`): on an item the actions of an item on it, on a list
  the actions of the collection. A client shows the buttons of the actions from it instead
  of a meta field of the product.
- A selector source may be a queryset (`get_selector_for_user` returning
  `Team.objects.filter(watchers=user)`), as the guide says: a foreign key or many-to-many
  selector failed with "The QuerySet value for an exact lookup must be limited to one
  result" (only `self` took it). It is read as a list of its objects.
- The guide describes the custom actions (the gap "public own action") and an operation of
  its own (a subclass of `AccessAction`).
- The helpers of the declarations (`discover`, `lock`, `migrations_complete`, the managed
  objects taken over by a declaration and `ManagedAdminMixin`) moved to the core,
  `bazis.core.declare` and `bazis.core.admin_abstract` (bazis 2.16), shared with
  bazis-statusy and the schedules of bazis-bg; `bazis.contrib.permit.declare` still gives
  `discover`, `lock` and `migrations_complete` for bazis-statusy < 2.12. The warning of a
  declaration that takes over an object is logged by `bazis.core.declare`.

## Deprecations

- `bazis.contrib.permit.declare.untranslated` is kept for bazis-statusy < 2.12, which
  imports it, and warns (`DeprecationWarning`); it is removed in bazis-permit 2.12.
